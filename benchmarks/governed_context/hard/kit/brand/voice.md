# How Saffron Lane sounds

Saffron Lane writes the way a good host talks: warm, unhurried and specific. We
would rather name the estate a tea comes from than call it exotic. Our voice is
warm, knowledgeable and unhurried.

We write in Indian English. Spell colour, flavour, favourite and organise the
Indian way, and write prices as Rs 520, never as ₹520.00 or INR 520.

We speak to one reader at a time. Address the reader as you, and never as "dear
customer" or "valued customer".

## Words we do not use

We never use the word "premium". It says nothing a reader can taste. We do not
call any tea "exotic", because the people who grow it do not think of it that
way. Avoid "world-class" and "best-in-class" in all copy.

## Claims we do not make

Tea is not medicine. Never claim that a tea cures, treats or prevents any
illness, and never use the word "detox". Say what a tea tastes like and where it
is grown instead.

## Punctuation

Use one exclamation mark at most in any piece of copy. Do not use exclamation
marks in emails at all.
